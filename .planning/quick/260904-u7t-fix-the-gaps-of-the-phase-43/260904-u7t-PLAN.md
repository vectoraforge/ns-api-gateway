<assumption_delta_decision>
**Signal:** `core.store_purchases.identity_value` holds two meanings — a server-minted `uuid7()`
placeholder and Apple's store-supplied `appAccountToken` — with no discriminator. That is the root
cause the review files as WR-06 and the reason CR-03 exists.

**Decision: promote, with no new column.** `resolved_token_value` is already the only-ever-store-supplied
value on that row: it is NULL by construction when the store gave no token, and the table's CHECK
allows no third value. Task 4 promotes it to be the key the attribution conflict guard reads.
`identity_value` keeps its NOT NULL placeholder duty and stops being asked a question it cannot answer.

**Rationale:** a discriminator column would be an `add-alongside` — new debt, a migration, and a second
thing to keep true — to express a fact the schema already expresses. No new noun is coined.

**What would force a later promote to a real column:** any future writer that sets
`resolved_token_value` for a purchase whose owner was not resolved from the store's own token. That
would break the column's one guarantee and the guard would lose its ground.

**Accepted residual, recorded not fixed:** the purchase row is still never updated
(`tables/purchases.py`: written once per lifecycle key), so a purchase first recorded unattributed
keeps its server-minted `identity_value` and its NULL `resolved_token_value` forever. The *subscription*
row does gain the owner and the buyer does get their grant, so the route recovers. Backfilling the
purchase row belongs to restore, and neither route does it today.
</assumption_delta_decision>
