<objective>
Ship the Google Play RTDN path end to end, thinnest path first: one Cloud Pub/Sub push with a
Google-signed OIDC token becomes one committed subscription row through the Phase 43 service.

Purpose: prove the architecture of a second store provider on one real path before any expansion
task builds on it. The promotion of `VerifiedNotification` out of `auth/app_store.py` and the
relocation of both providers' status and product maps into their own classes is the architectural
decision this slice proves; it cannot be deferred without sending the Google path through the
date-derived status the phase deletes.

envelope model, the two `app.state` classes, `verify_google_play_notification`, the second route
on `webhooks_router`, and the e2e case that proves the whole path.

<assumption_delta_decision>
**Signal:** `pluralization` — "the second and last provider-callback route". A second store
provider arrives where the code had one.

**The noun that is now primary:** the verified store notification, not the App Store notification.

**Decision: `promote`.** `VerifiedNotification` moves out of `auth/app_store.py` into
`auth/store_notifications.py` (D-06) and becomes the general primary; Apple becomes one variant
that fills it, exactly as Google does. The status word (D-11) and the product-to-tier map (D-16)
move out of `services/subscriptions.py` into each provider's own class, so the service holds no
provider-specific rule at all. Nothing is added alongside a still-Apple-shaped primary.

**Invariant test suggested (accepted, planned):** the parametrised state-map cases in plan 44-03
and the Apple one-to-one case in plan 44-05 together assert that every supported provider variant
produces a finished `SubscriptionStatus` in its own class, so a future phase that reintroduces a
date-derived status in the service goes red.
</assumption_delta_decision>

  <name>Checkpoint: the Google deduplication key (OQ-4) — a one-way door</name>
  <decision>What value fills `VerifiedNotification.notification_uuid` on the Google path?</decision>
  <context>
    `44-CONTEXT.md` never says. Apple supplies `notificationUUID` in its signed envelope; Google's
    `DeveloperNotification` carries `version`, `packageName`, `eventTimeMillis` and the
    notification bodies, and none of them is a delivery id (RESEARCH F-10, OQ-4).
    `audit.subscription_events.notification_uuid` is `UNIQUE NOT NULL` and is the only thing that
    stops a Pub/Sub redelivery re-writing (`services/subscriptions.py:77`).

    **This is a one-way door.** Once Google rows carry a key, changing the scheme is a data
    migration of `audit.subscription_events` plus a window in which old and new keys do not dedupe
    against each other. Phase 45 (restore) writes through the same column.

    The purchase token is already persisted as `external_id` under the D-10 flagged divergence, and
    `notification_uuid` reaches no log line in `services/subscriptions.py` or `crud/subscriptions.py`
    (grepped: the three logger calls carry `event_type` and `provider` only).
  </context>
  <options>
    <option id="composite">
      <name>Payload-derived composite — `google_play:{purchaseToken}:{eventTimeMillis}:{notificationType}` (recommended)</name>
      <pros>Deterministic from the RTDN itself, so it dedupes a Pub/Sub redelivery AND a Play republish of the same event. Depends on no unstated Google guarantee. Provider prefix makes an Apple UUID and a Google key non-colliding in the shared UNIQUE index. Readable in a database row during an incident.</pros>
      <cons>It is this project's construction, not the store's. It writes the purchase token into a second column.</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="hashed">
      <name>Hashed composite — `google_play:{sha256 of the same three values}`</name>
      <pros>Same dedupe properties; the purchase token appears in one column only.</pros>
      <cons>Opaque during an incident — the row cannot be traced back to a delivery without recomputing. Adds a construction step no source artifact asks for.</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="messageid">
      <name>`message.messageId` from the Pub/Sub envelope</name>
      <pros>Simplest; a real unique id within the topic; D-04 already requires it to be present.</pros>
      <cons>Rests on assumption A1 — Google's reference says only that `messageId` is "assigned by the server when the message is published" and does NOT state stability across redeliveries. If it changes per delivery, the replay guard misses silently: a second audit row lands with no UNIQUE violation and a duplicate grant write runs.</cons>
    </option>
  </options>
