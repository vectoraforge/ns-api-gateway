## Summary

**Runtime core** (part 1, 18 files — 1C/6W/4I)

The runtime core is in good shape structurally: the error tree is total and its totality is
enforced by a test with seven negative controls, the exception-handler layer produces exactly one
log line per rejection with the exception it was given rather than the ambient one, the lifespan's
`finally` guards every shutdown step individually, and `test_app_wiring.py` pins the
authenticated/public/callback partition off the live router rather than off a literal. The 37.5
review's WR-02/WR-03 (breaker moved off the attempt) and CR-01 (admission holding a DB connection)
are genuinely closed by phase 41's D-14/D-15 and are not re-flagged. `ruff check src tests` and
`.venv/bin/pytest tests/unit` are clean (1562 passed); `ty check src` reports three diagnostics,
all in `auth/devicecheck.py`, outside this partition.

The one blocker is a hole D-14 and D-15 opened together and neither considered: the quota charge is
committed on a circuit-breaker verdict that is re-evaluated after an unbounded wait for a provider
permit, so a request can be billed a monthly credit and then refused with 503 having made zero
provider calls. I reproduced it. That contradicts 37.5 D-07's invariant verbatim and D-15's own
stated property. The warnings are a mix of fail-closed asymmetry at boot (`JWTVerifier` is the one
JWKS consumer constructed unguarded), a PII channel that opens at `log_level: DEBUG`, a config-merge
crash that names no file, an OpenAPI contract that documents neither 403 nor 409, and a coverage
gap: no unit test executes the production `get_db` at all.

**Auth adapters** (part 2, 19 files — 1C/5W/3I)

The authentication and store-integration seam is in good shape on the properties that matter most:
no path in `jwt_verifier.py`, `app_store.py`, `google_play.py`, `devicecheck.py` or `firebase.py`
accepts an unverified credential, and every failure arm I traced ends in a raise or a
`(None, reason)` pair. The Pub/Sub push token is pinned on signature, issuer, `aud`,
service-account `email` and `email_verified` exactly as `09-webhook-google-play-rtdn.md` requires;
the Apple envelope and both nested payloads are each verified on their own as `08-webhook-app-store.md`
requires; the JWKS negative cache is bounded, locked and negative-only. Four candidate findings were
dropped as already ratified: the composite Google replay key (44 OQ-4), the always-registered
callback routes (43 D-02), the shared error body on a "plain status" route
(`REQUIREMENTS.md:382`/`:432`), and the certificate window resting on the claimed `signedDate`
with online checks off (P-09, asserted in `test_app_store_notifications.py:568-589`).

What is wrong is concentrated in the exception surfaces. One proven defect turns a mounted-wrong
DeviceCheck key into a crashloop of the whole pod, which is the precise outcome `read_private_key`
was written to prevent. Two more let a store payload escape its seam as a generic 500 where the
Apple path lacks a guard its Google sibling already carries and documents. The remaining findings
are seam-annotation and schema-bound gaps of the same kind an earlier pass already fixed on the
other provider. All 1562 unit tests pass and `ruff check src tests` is clean; `ty check src` reports
three diagnostics and all three are in this partition.

**Persistence** (part 3, 34 files — 1C/7W/6I)

The persistence layer is careful and unusually well documented: the migration DDL and the
SQLModel column sets agree table for table, the lock order (grant rows ascending by id, then
their usage rows) is written once and pinned to production by compiled-SQL mirrors, and the
IntegrityError narrowing reads SQLSTATE only, with a live driver test proving the attribute is
there. The concurrency suites are real races on real connections, with premise assertions that
stop most of them from passing vacuously. Two structural gaps remain. First, `SubscriptionsDB`
mixes column selects (`read_owner`, `read_status`, written specifically to defeat the SQLAlchemy
identity map) with an entity load (`read_subscription`) that does not, and the store-clock
out-of-order guard plus the whole owner/replay decision in `upsert_subscription` are built on
that stale instance — under two concurrent deliveries an older payload downgrades a paying
subscriber, which is the exact defect the guard exists to prevent. Second, the schema suite
asserts tables, enums, indexes and index predicates as exact sets but never asserts a single
foreign key's ON DELETE action, so two of the four cascades `00-schema.md:621` enumerates could
be silently dropped and the suite would stay green. Three test classes also assert invariants
they structurally cannot detect, or depend on collection order in a session-scoped scratch
database seven modules commit into.

**Services** (part 4, 27 files — 1C/6W/6I)

The service layer is disciplined: one captured instant per request, one written lock order, remote
calls placed strictly outside open transactions, and fail-closed branches wherever a read is
missing. `pytest tests/unit` (1562 cases), `ruff check src tests` and `ty check src` are clean for
this partition. The defects that remain are in the arithmetic and in the mixing of two sources of
truth, not in the plumbing. The sharpest is `RestoreService.restore`, which reads the entitlement
*status* off the canonical row but derives the entitlement *window* from the client's proof; on
Apple the two can never agree for a subscription in `grace_period`, so a paying subscriber in grace
is told "no active subscription found". Around it sit two quota-accounting defects — the monthly
period can roll backwards at a UTC month boundary and erase a committed charge, and the
subscription grant writer mints a fresh `monthly_used = 0` counter on every term change, so a
grace-period bounce resets a paid subscriber's allowance mid-month. One device-bit write on the
anonymous claim still lacks the guard its registered sibling got in 37.4 WR-64. On the test side,
`test_subscription_attribution.py` drives the service through a hand-written re-implementation of
the subscription crud that has already drifted from it, and the service's own out-of-order
("superseded") branch has no unit case at all.

**HTTP surface** (part 5, 15 files — 0C/6W/5I)

The HTTP surface is in good shape on the two things that matter most: every handler is
`Depends()`-only, no handler constructs a database class, and no handler trusts a
client-supplied identifier for ownership — `chat_id` is always paired with
`identity.user.id`, the challenge routes bind on `core.external_identities.id`, and both

is genuinely pinned by `tests/unit/test_app_wiring.py`, so a new route with no verifier
cannot slip through. I found no Critical defect: nothing in the routers grants entitlement,
leaks an internal field, or answers before its rows are durable.

What I did find is a cluster of consistency failures, most of them inside `routers/auth.py`,
where six phases each added a route and each solved the same problem a different way.
`issue_challenge` is the only handler body in `src/` that reads the clock directly instead of
taking `Depends(get_evaluated_at)` — the identical finding was raised twice against
`get_chat_service` and fixed in 37.4, and this instance was missed. The restore route writes
two structured log lines for one rejection where `SHARED-INVARIANTS.md` § Fail-closed defaults
demands exactly one, and its sibling 40 lines above gets it right. `POST /auth/challenge`
is the only route that returns a raw `JSONResponse`, so it publishes no response schema at
all — while a comment 55 lines below it explains precisely why that is the wrong shape. On
the Google Play callback, the ratified "200 and log it, never 422" decision was landed only
for the length bound; a push whose `message` carries no `data` still answers 422 and
redelivers forever.

**Packaging & deployment** (part 6, 30 files — 1C/8W/5I)

The packaging and deployment surface is in much better shape than the phase-37 reviews left it: the

## Critical Issues

### CR-01: A request is charged a monthly credit and then refused 503 without ever reaching the provider

**File:** `src/nativespeaker/api/resilience.py:137-142,144-182` (with `src/nativespeaker/api/services/chats.py:95-98` and `:124-127` as the caller)

**Issue:**

`admission()` consults the breaker and takes an in-flight slot, both instantaneous. `ChatService`
then commits the quota charge inside that context. Only afterwards does `ainvoke()` wait on
`self._gate.concurrency()` — a plain `async with self._semaphore` with no bound — and only after
that permit is granted does `attempt()` re-consult the breaker (`resilience.py:151`, phase 41 D-14).

Between the charge and that re-consultation, other requests can trip the breaker. When they do, the
waiting request's first `attempt()` raises `CircuitOpenError` at `resilience.py:151`, which
`resilience.py:153-155` re-raises unwrapped and `_should_retry` declines, so it reaches the client
as a 503 `service_unavailable` — with a monthly credit already spent and no provider call made.

Reproduced against the installed code (`pool_size=1`, `queue_size=5`, `threshold=1`,
`retry_max_attempts=1`), charging at exactly the point `ChatService` charges:

```
A -> TransientLLMError
B -> CircuitOpenError
charges committed: ['A', 'B']
provider calls made: ['A']
```

At the shipped configuration (`config/config.yaml`: `pool_size: 5`, `queue_size: 25`,
`circuit_breaker_failure_threshold: 5`) this is not a corner case — it is the *normal* shape of a
provider outage. Thirty requests admit and charge; five hold permits; the first wave's failures open
the breaker within one attempt round; the remaining twenty-five have each already spent a credit and
every one of them gets a 503 that never touched OpenAI. On a sub-$5/month product where the monthly
allowance *is* the entitlement, that is silent revenue-negative billing during an incident, and the
quota code has no refund path by design.

This is not a re-litigation of a ratified decision but a gap in one:

- **37.5 D-07** states the invariant verbatim: *"A user is never charged for a request that did not
  reach the provider."* This path violates it.
- **41 D-15** states the property it believed it preserved: *"an open breaker or a full queue still
  answers 503 having spent nothing."* That is true only of the breaker check inside `admission()`;
  it is false of the one D-14 put inside `attempt()`.
- **41 D-14** contemplated the charged-and-not-refunded case, but explicitly as *"already true when
  all three attempts fail"* — i.e. for a request that did reach the provider. The zero-attempt case
  is new, and it is created by D-14 and D-15 landing in the same phase.

`tests/unit/test_quota_seam.py`'s twenty billing cases assert that an open circuit and a full queue
leave `usage.monthly_used == 0`, but they all exercise the breaker at *admission*. None of them
opens the breaker while a charged request waits for a permit, so the suite is green through this.

**Fix:**

Root cause: the charge is committed against a breaker verdict that is re-evaluated later, across an
unbounded wait. Two verdicts, one charge. Pick one of these — the first keeps D-07's invariant as
written, the second amends it honestly:

1. **Make the zero-attempt refusal refundable.** Have `attempt()` distinguish a breaker refusal on
   the *first* attempt (no provider call made) from one after an attempt, and have `ChatService`
   credit the unit back for the former. This is the only option that keeps D-07 literally true. It
   needs a compensating write in `QuotaService`, which the module deliberately lacks today, so it is
   the larger change.

2. **Move the charge behind the permit and re-check.** Have `ResiliencePolicy` expose the permit as
   its own instantaneous-once-granted context so `ChatService` reads:

   ```python
   async with self.llm_service.admission() as admitted:      # breaker + in-flight slot
       async with self.llm_service.provider_permit(admitted) as ticket:  # semaphore, then breaker re-check
           await self.quota_service.charge(user_id=user_id, evaluated_at=self.evaluated_at)
           ai_message = await self.ask_llm(chat, human_message, ticket)
   ```

   No callback chain is reinstated (D-07's prohibition holds; the charge stays a plain call at a
   visible point). The cost is exactly what D-15 removed: the charge's database round trip again
   runs under a provider permit. That trade is now measurable rather than theoretical, because
   `db.pool_size` is 12 against `resilience.pool_size` 5 (41 D-16), so the pool can no longer be the
   thing that stalls.

Whichever is chosen, amend `AGENTS.md` § Resilience and the D-07 wording so the written invariant
and the code agree, and add the missing case to `tests/unit/test_quota_seam.py`: open the breaker
while a charged request waits on the semaphore, and assert `monthly_used`.

### CR-09: `read_private_key` lets two exception types escape and crashloops the pod

**File:** `src/nativespeaker/api/auth/devicecheck.py:54-71` (the `except` tuple at `:69`)

**Issue:** The function's contract is stated twice — the signature says
`-> str | None`, the docstring says "or return `None` when there is no usable key there", and the
comment at `:63-71` says the whole point is that "a key that is present but unusable is the same
absent state an unset path is: a 503 on the claim and one boot warning". It catches
`(OSError, ValueError, jwt.PyJWTError)`. `jwt.encode(..., algorithm="ES256")` raises two types
outside that tuple, both proven against the installed `PyJWT 2.12.1` and `cryptography` in `.venv`:

```
ec-public-key            -> ESCAPED AttributeError 'ECPublicKey' object has no attribute 'sign'
encrypted-ec-private-key -> ESCAPED TypeError Password was not given but private key is encrypted
```

(Confirmed by calling `read_private_key()` directly on a temp file holding each form.)
`PyJWT`'s `ECAlgorithm.prepare_key` tries `load_pem_public_key` first and only falls back to
`load_pem_private_key(key_bytes, password=None)` on `ValueError`; a public-key PEM therefore parses
successfully and only fails later at `.sign()` (`AttributeError`), and an encrypted PKCS#8 PEM
fails the fallback with `TypeError`, not `ValueError`.

Concrete failure: an operator mounts the public half of the DeviceCheck key pair (or a
passphrase-wrapped `.p8`) at `devicecheck.private_key_path`. `lifespan` calls `read_private_key` at
`app/lifespan.py:129` inside a `try:` whose only handler is `finally:`, so the exception propagates
out of the lifespan context manager, ASGI startup fails, and the container restarts — forever.
Instead of one warning line and a 503 on the anonymous grant claim, the **entire API is down**:
`/chats`, `/auth/sync`, `/users/me` and both webhooks included. This is exactly the regression the
comment at `:63-71` and the WR-22 fix it cites were written to close, and the test matrix at
`tests/unit/test_devicecheck_adapter.py:258-286` misses both cases — it covers garbage, empty,
framing-only, non-text and RSA, all of which do land inside the caught tuple.

**Fix:** Catch every failure the parse probe can produce, since the function's entire job is to
convert "unusable key" into `None`:

```python
    try:
        text = pem.read_text()
        jwt.encode({}, text, algorithm="ES256")
    except Exception:
        # Every unusable key is one outcome: the same absent state an unset path is. The exception
        # is dropped rather than logged: its text quotes the file's own bytes.
        return None
    return text
```

A blanket `except Exception` is correct here precisely because the function is a classifier with
two outcomes and no caller can act on the distinction. Add the two proven forms — an EC **public**
key PEM and a `BestAvailableEncryption` PKCS#8 PEM — to the parametrisation at
`tests/unit/test_devicecheck_adapter.py:258-263`, which is what stops a third recurrence.

### CR-17: `read_subscription` hands back a stale ORM instance, so a concurrent older store payload downgrades a paying subscriber

**File:** `src/nativespeaker/api/crud/subscriptions.py:99-102,164-205` (call sites `src/nativespeaker/api/services/subscriptions.py:51-52,79-94,124-131`)

**Issue:** `read_subscription` is a plain entity load. SQLAlchemy returns an instance already in the
session's identity map **without refreshing its attributes**, so the second call inside
`upsert_subscription:173` re-reads nothing — it hands back the object loaded at
`services/subscriptions.py:51`, carrying the values that read saw. This module already knows this:
`read_owner:106-107` and `read_status:115-116` are column selects for exactly this reason
("the identity map would answer a row already loaded with the value that read saw"). The
out-of-order store-clock guard and `upsert_subscription` were not given the same treatment.

Concrete failure, entirely within one buyer:

1. Apple delivers `DID_RENEW` (A, `signed_at = T2`, status `active`) and a stale `EXPIRED`
   (B, `signed_at = T1 < T2`, status `expired`) at the same time.
2. Both run `read_subscription` (`services/subscriptions.py:51`) before either commits, so both
   hold the snapshot `S0` where `store_signed_at = T0 < T1`.
3. Both call `lock_grants(owner)` (`crud/subscriptions.py:77-83`). They take the same buyer's
   grant rows `FOR UPDATE`, so B blocks until A commits. A commits `status = active`,
   `store_signed_at = T2`, and the renewed grant.
4. B resumes. `read_owner` (`:66`) is a fresh column select and sees the unchanged owner, so the
   `settled_owner` guard passes. `read_event` finds no row for B's own `notification_uuid`.
5. The out-of-order guard at `services/subscriptions.py:79-81` compares `notification.signed_at`
   (`T1`) against `stored.store_signed_at` — still `T0` from `S0`, never `T2`. `T1 > T0`, so the
   guard does not fire.
6. `upsert_subscription` re-reads and gets the same stale object: `(stored.tier_id,
   stored.status, stored.user_id)` is the pre-A tuple, the replay branch is missed, and the UPDATE
   writes `status = expired`, `store_signed_at = T1`.
7. `write_subscription_grant` sees `entitled = False`, supersedes A's freshly renewed grant
   (`:294-319`), and the commit succeeds — no unique index is violated on this path, so nothing
   catches it.

The paying customer's subscription row reads `expired` and their grant is expired, from a payload

### CR-25: An Apple restore of a subscription in `grace_period` is always refused, because the status is read off the row and the term off the proof

**File:** `src/nativespeaker/api/services/restore.py:58-76` (with `src/nativespeaker/api/auth/app_store.py:154-164`)

**Issue:** `restore` decides entitlement from two different sources and then requires them to agree:

```python
status = proof.status if stored is None else stored.status      # the canonical row's word
...
term_ends_at = term_end_for(status, proof)                      # the proof's dates
if term_ends_at is None or term_ends_at <= self.evaluated_at:
    raise RestoreSubscriptionNotEntitled
```

`term_end_for` reads `term.grace_period_expires_at` when the status is `grace_period` and
`term.expires_at` otherwise (`auth/store_notifications.py:76-81`). But
`AppStoreNotifications.verify_transaction` constructs `RestoredSubscription(...,
grace_period_expires_at=None)` unconditionally — Apple's grace window lives in the renewal payload,
which a client-presented signed transaction does not carry (`auth/app_store.py:163-164`, and the
unit case `test_restore_proof.py:145` pins that this field is unreachable from a proof).

Concrete failure: a subscriber's card fails, Apple grants a billing grace period and the webhook
records `core.subscriptions.status = 'grace_period'` (`_APPLE_STATUSES` maps
`Status.BILLING_GRACE_PERIOD` at `auth/app_store.py:25`, so this row state is reachable). The
subscriber reinstalls and calls `POST /auth/restore-subscription` with a valid signed transaction.
`status` is `grace_period`, which passes `ENTITLED_STATUSES`; `term_ends_at` is
`proof.grace_period_expires_at`, which is `None`; line 72 raises `RestoreSubscriptionNotEntitled`
→ the client is told "no active subscription found — cannot be restored". A paying customer is
denied the entitlement they hold. `10-restore-subscription.md:65` requires exactly the opposite:
"Confirm product-entitled under locked state (statuses exactly `active` and `grace_period` …)".
The Play path is unaffected, because `read_for_restore` fills
`grace_period_expires_at=expiry if in_grace else None` — so this is an Apple-only wrong answer, and
one no unit case covers (`test_restore_proof.py:765-772` only exercises a status that *moved*
under the locks, which masks the stable-grace case).

The same mix produces a second wrong 403 on the ordinary path: a StoreKit client that presents the
subscription's *original* signed transaction (the only artifact available for an original whose own
term has long expired) sends a proof whose `expiresDate` is the first term's. `status` is `active`
from the canonical row, `term_ends_at` is that stale expiry, `term_ends_at <= evaluated_at`, and the
restore is refused for a customer who is currently subscribed.

D-06 settled that "a row that exists decides with its own status". It did not settle that the
*window* may come from a different artifact than the status; the comment at line 64 states the mix
as if it were free, and it is not.

**Fix:** Take the status and the term from the same source. When a canonical row exists, take both
from it — it already carries the term the webhook wrote, and the grant writer is fed exactly that:

```python
if stored is None:
    status, term_ends_at = proof.status, term_end_for(proof.status, proof)
else:
    # Canonical state is the webhooks': the status and the window it was recorded with travel together.
    status = stored.status
    term_ends_at = await self.subscriptions_db.read_term_end(stored)   # or the row's own column
if status not in ENTITLED_STATUSES:
    raise RestoreSubscriptionNotEntitled
if term_ends_at is None or term_ends_at <= self.evaluated_at:
    raise RestoreSubscriptionNotEntitled
```

`core.subscriptions` does not currently carry the term end, so the minimum change is to persist it
on ingestion (the value `write_subscription_grant` already receives as `ends_at`) and read it back
here. If that is too large for this phase, the narrow fix is to fall back to the proof's paid
expiry when the row says `grace_period` and the proof carries no grace window — Apple's grace
window always ends at or after the paid expiry, so the resulting grant is never longer than the one
the webhook wrote, and the customer is not refused.

### WR-16: `_instant` has no range guard, so one out-of-range Apple stamp 500s the webhook forever

**File:** `src/nativespeaker/api/auth/app_store.py:37-39` (callers at `:47`, `:69-72`, `:162-163`)

**Issue:** `_instant` calls `datetime.fromtimestamp(milliseconds / 1000, UTC)` with no guard. The
library models every Apple stamp as an unbounded `Optional[int]` and its cattrs converter accepts
any integer (measured: `{"expiresDate": 10**18}` structures to `1000000000000000000` without
complaint). Measured against the installed code:

```
_instant(10**18)          -> RAISED ValueError year must be in 1..9999, not 31690708
_instant(253402300800000) -> RAISED ValueError year must be in 1..9999, not 10000
_instant(-10**18)         -> RAISED ValueError year must be in 1..9999, not -31686769
```

A `ValueError` from `_instant` escapes `AppStoreNotifications.verify`, escapes

the generic 500 handler. Per `08-webhook-app-store.md` a 5xx is what makes Apple retry, so Apple

subscription event is never recorded and the operator sees an unexplained 500 loop.

### WR-17: the seam converts only `VerificationException`, so a structurally invalid payload is a 500 where the spec requires 401

**File:** `src/nativespeaker/api/auth/app_store.py:90-93, 108-111, 132-135, 145-149`

**Issue:** All four library calls are wrapped in `except VerificationException`. But

The same second arm belongs on `:108-111`, `:132-135` and — as `ProofRejected`, matching the

**Issue:** `data` is the one string in the entire request-schema surface with no `max_length`. The
comment at `:28-31` justifies it: a body pydantic refuses is a 422, and Pub/Sub acknowledges 2xx
alone, so a 422 loops forever — hence the bound moved into `developer_notification_from`
(`google_play.py:134-139`), which answers 200 and drops. That reasoning is right for a *slightly*
oversized RTDN and I am not asking to reverse it.

It does not hold at the other magnitude. `/webhooks/google-play/rtdn` sits outside the barrier
(`08`/`09`: provider-callback, no Firebase JWT), and FastAPI reads and JSON-parses the **entire**
body in `get_request_handler` *before* `solve_dependencies` runs — so the token check inside
`verify_google_play_notification` (`app/dependencies.py:200-210`) happens strictly after the body is
already in memory. An unauthenticated caller who never presents a bearer token can therefore make
the pod buffer and parse an arbitrarily large `{"message":{"data":"AAAA…"}}` body. There is no
body-size middleware in the app (`app/main.py` registers only `RequestLoggingMiddleware`) and Envoy
does not bound request bodies by default — it bounds JWTs and rate, which is what `AGENTS.md` says
to leave to it. Rate limiting does not help here: one request is enough.

orders of magnitude above any real delivery costs nothing and keeps the redelivery argument intact —
no genuine Pub/Sub push can reach it, so no genuine push turns into a 422 loop.

**Fix:**

```python
# below a body worth buffering: this route is outside the barrier and FastAPI parses the body
# before the push token is checked. `PUBSUB_DATA_LIMIT` still decides the 200-and-drop arm.
PUBSUB_ENVELOPE_LIMIT = 1_048_576

class PubSubPushMessage(BaseModel):
    data: str = Field(..., max_length=PUBSUB_ENVELOPE_LIMIT)
```

`AppStoreNotificationRequest.signedPayload` (`:17`) already carries a bound, but note it is enforced
after the same full parse — if the fix lands as a shared body-size guard instead, both routes get it.

### WR-47: The monthly period rolls backwards at the UTC month boundary and erases a committed charge

**File:** `src/nativespeaker/api/services/quota.py:73-78`

**Issue:**

notification and gets the identical 500 every time, and because the raise happens before
`append_event`, the `notification_uuid` idempotency key is never written, so no later delivery can
short-circuit either.

Concrete: Google Play's `subscriptionsv2.get` is read live in
`verify_google_play_notification` and `_status_for` maps `SUBSCRIPTION_STATE_ACTIVE` to `active`
from Google's state word alone, without consulting the expiry
(`auth/google_play.py:196-204`). Google's state and its `lineItems[].expiryTime` are eventually
consistent, so a read can legitimately answer `ACTIVE` with an expiry a few seconds in the past.
That RTDN then 500s, Pub/Sub redelivers it on backoff for up to seven days, every redelivery re-runs
the live Play read and the whole verification, and the notification is never recorded. The same
shape reaches Apple on any first delivery of a term that closed before the delivery landed, where
the out-of-order guard cannot help because there is no stored `store_signed_at` yet.

**Fix:** Record the delivery and decline the grant, rather than refusing the whole transaction:

```python
if status in ENTITLED_STATUSES and (term_ends_at is None or term_ends_at <= self.evaluated_at):
    # The store's word and its own window disagree: the row and the event are recorded, and no
    # grant is written, so the redelivery is deduped by `notification_uuid` instead of 500ing again.
    logger.error("store_notification_without_term", event_type=notification.event_type)
    term_ends_at, status = term_ends_at, SubscriptionStatus.expired
```

or, minimally, append the event row and commit before raising, so the second delivery takes the
replay arm at line 75 instead of the raise.

```python
                       and isinstance(node.value, ast.List | ast.Set | ast.Dict | ast.Tuple)]
```

```python
                collections.append(node)
```

### IN-43: `tests/e2e` mixes relative and absolute imports of its own conftest
