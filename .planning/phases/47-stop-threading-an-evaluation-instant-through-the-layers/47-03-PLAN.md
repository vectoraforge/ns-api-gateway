<objective>
The two free-grant writers and the two identity writers stop being told what time it is. Each reads
the clock once, at the top of its own method, and stamps every column of that write from that one
read.

This is the phase's central pattern (PATTERNS Cluster 3) applied to the nine-use and ten-use methods
where it matters most: `activate_anonymous_device_grant` stamps a grant row, a usage row and an
identity marker from one value, and two e2e cases assert two of those columns are equal to each
other. A read per assignment would break them. A read per method keeps them exactly true.

**Adopted from RESEARCH Open Question 2, as written:** each crud writer reads its own clock rather
than taking one from its service. The accepted consequence is named and nothing is built to prevent
it — on `activate_registered_account_grant` the superseded grant's `ends_at` and the new grant's
`starts_at` come from the same single read and stay equal, but across a *service* that calls several
writers the reads differ by microseconds, so a superseded grant can carry an `ends_at` a hair before
the replacement's `starts_at`. That is harmless: the superseded row's `status` is already `expired`
and the effective-grant predicate filters on status first. Do not add machinery to close the window.

Purpose: criterion 2 for the grant and identity write paths, with criterion 3's "one read at the point
of use" honored per method rather than per statement.
Output: four writer methods that take no datetime, their four service call sites, and the schema and
unit cases that drove them.

**Reversibility:** `reversible` — four signatures and their call sites, all inside this repository,
with no stored data changing shape.

**Executor note.** `IS_WORKTREE` is **false** in this repository: GSD's detector reports true because
this repo is a submodule whose `.git` is a file, and believing it makes tracking writes vanish.
</objective>
